# Funding and Collaboration

## Turning the research programme into an empirically validated body of work

Information Asymmetry is an early-stage research programme investigating whether higher-level psychological descriptions compress deeper dynamical structure, and whether that compression becomes causally insufficient for tasks such as explanation, intervention, incident reconstruction, governance, accountability, and prevention.

A central applied question is:

> **What minimum causal resolution must an explanation preserve to be sufficient for the task at hand?**

The accompanying Psychological-Language Absurdity Test sharpens this into a practical diagnostic for AI systems that take actions:

> **The Absurdity Test does not ask whether psychological language is allowed. It asks whether psychological language is causally sufficient.**

This repository already contains a formal preprint, mathematical notes, experimental protocols, engineering evidence, worked examples, and a live structural governance implementation. The next objective is to turn that foundation into a rigorous, externally reviewed and empirically tested research programme.

## What exists today

The current programme includes:

- a formal Information Asymmetry hypothesis;
- a publication-ready preprint;
- a Psychological-Language Absurdity Test;
- a causal-sufficiency framing for action-taking AI systems;
- mathematical notes on state space, reachability, constraint geometry, information loss, forward mapping, and reverse reconstruction;
- experimental templates for testing retained and lost information;
- an engineering implementation based on state, trajectory, reachability, constraints, and pre-execution intervention;
- worked examples across AI, aviation, medical devices, guided systems, nuclear safety, railway signalling, cybersecurity, finance, and other complex systems.

Runtime Governance is an engineering implementation inspired by the structural framework. It is **not** presented as proof of the scientific hypothesis.

## What needs to happen next

The research now needs independent empirical pressure.

Priority directions include:

1. **Formalise causal sufficiency**
   - Define what it means for a description to preserve enough causal structure for a specified task.
   - Distinguish descriptive usefulness from causal adequacy.
   - Investigate whether a minimum causal resolution can be defined operationally.

2. **Design comparative experiments**
   - Compare psychological descriptions with higher-resolution dynamical descriptions.
   - Measure reconstruction quality, prediction, intervention selection, incident understanding, and responsibility attribution.
   - Test whether different descriptions preserve different amounts of control-relevant information.

3. **Build evaluation benchmarks**
   - Create repeatable tasks in which participants or systems must reconstruct an event from different representational resolutions.
   - Measure information retained, information lost, ambiguity, intervention quality, and causal error.

4. **Test action-taking AI systems**
   - Study AI agents and tool-using systems in controlled environments.
   - Examine state, inputs, capabilities, permissions, constraints, reachable actions, transitions, execution paths, and intervention points.
   - Test whether psychological summaries are sufficient for incident analysis and governance tasks.

5. **Independent review and replication**
   - Invite critique from researchers in AI safety, causal inference, dynamical systems, cognitive science, philosophy of science, human factors, systems engineering, and governance.
   - Replicate experiments across models, architectures, tasks, and domains.

## A possible research programme

A mature programme could be organised around three connected tracks:

### 1. Theory

Formalise Information Asymmetry, causal sufficiency, reconstruction loss, representational compression, reachability, intervention-sensitive structure, and minimum causal resolution.

### 2. Empirical validation

Run controlled studies that test whether higher-resolution dynamical representations support better reconstruction, intervention, prediction, accountability, or prevention than compressed psychological descriptions.

### 3. Applied AI safety and governance

Evaluate action-taking AI systems using causal accounts that expose state, inputs, capabilities, permissions, constraints, reachable states, transitions, execution paths, and intervention points.

The long-term question is whether this can support a more rigorous explanatory standard for AI incidents and action-taking systems.

## Research standard being explored

A working formulation is:

> **An explanation is causally sufficient for a specified task only if it preserves enough relevant structure to reconstruct the transition, identify meaningful intervention points, locate control failures, and support the intended causal task — such as explanation, governance, accountability, or recurrence prevention.**

This is a research proposition to be tested and refined, not a finished standard.

## Why this matters

Anthropomorphic or psychological language may be perfectly useful in ordinary communication.

The concern arises when a compressed description substitutes for a causal account in settings where consequences are real and causal accountability matters.

If an explanation would be rejected as insufficient in aviation, medical-device safety, nuclear protection, guided systems, railway signalling, or another safety-critical domain, the same question should be asked when an AI system takes actions.

The central issue is therefore not whether psychological language is permitted.

It is whether the explanation is **causally sufficient for the task being performed**.
